Reading

How I read the role

The posting asks for a seller who can originate large experience transformation deals inside accounts that other people own. Wipro has posted four roles like it in four months, one for each cluster of industries, at a time when its Americas revenue is shrinking and its large-deal bookings fell by more than half in a quarter. The role is a growth bet, and the people hiring will judge it on pipeline within two quarters.

Wipro brings delivery scale, a design company in Designit, strong Salesforce, Pega and ServiceNow practices, and AI platforms under Wipro Intelligence. Analysts mark it down for its thin creative and onshore presence at the front of a deal. A seller who can lead the design and advisory conversation with a CMO, then hand over to Wipro's delivery model with a credible plan, addresses that weakness directly.

That is the case I make on these pages. I have sold design-led consulting to Fortune 500 executives for sixteen years, built a new service line inside a matrix of studios, and won multi-year portfolio work in life sciences. I have not sold DX platform services, and the Fit page says so and sets out how I would close that gap.

Approach

Three ideas behind the plan

Idea 1

Client partners are the first customer

The practitioner does not own accounts. Every opportunity starts by giving a client partner a reason to open the door: a dated trigger, a point of view and a sized idea that grows their number.

Idea 2

Sell the deadline

AEM 6.5 on-premises support ends in February 2027. The ADA Title II web rule applies from April 2027, and HHS Section 504 from May 2027. A dated event turns a capability pitch into a decision the client has to make.

Idea 3

Land with design, expand to run

A Designit-led diagnostic under $1m opens the door. The build and the multi-year managed run that follow make it a $20m deal, and they play to Wipro's delivery scale.
