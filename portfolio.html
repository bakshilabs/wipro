Book

Three parts of the book

I would sort every account in the portfolio into one of three groups in the first month, because each group needs a different kind of selling and a different pipeline coverage.

Protect

Live DX work in the book

Renewals and phase extensions on work Wipro already delivers. The aim is zero leakage: every live engagement has a named next phase and a date for proposing it.

Expand

SMU accounts with no DX yet

Accounts where Wipro runs infrastructure, applications or operations but the marketing and digital teams buy from an agency. Wipro's existing access to the CIO shortens the route to the CDO.

New logo

Clients with a dated trigger

A short list of companies with a forced event, such as a platform end-of-support date, a spin-off or a new CMO. These take longer and need a sharper point of view.

Rhythm

Operating rhythm

Matrix selling depends on regular meetings with people who do not report to me. This is the cadence I would set up in the first 30 days.

Operating rhythm
CadenceWhat happensWho
WeeklyPipeline review of every opportunity above $1m: next step, date, owner, risk.Me, practice lead
FortnightlyAccount sessions with each priority SMU client partner on triggers and joint plays.Me, client partners
MonthlyAlliance pipeline review with Adobe, Salesforce, SAP and Pega partner managers, matched against their registered opportunities.Me, alliance managers
QuarterlyBook review: bookings against target, coverage for the next two quarters, and qualify-out decisions.Me, DX leadership
Each large pursuitDeal reviews at qualification, solution and price, with the leadership sponsor briefed before client meetings.Pursuit team, deal desk

Assumptions

Win rates, deal sizes and meeting ratios are working assumptions drawn from my own pipeline experience in consulting. They are not Wipro data. First-year revenue assumes bookings arrive evenly through the year and bill evenly over the contract.
